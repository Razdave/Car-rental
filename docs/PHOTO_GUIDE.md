# Fleet photo guide

How to shoot neat, consistent photos of every Aurevon car at your car stand and on Nigerian roads, then put them in the app.
A recent phone (iPhone 12 or newer, Samsung Galaxy S/A series) is enough — no professional camera needed.

## 1. Before the shoot

- **Wash and detail the car** the same morning: body, wheels, tyre shine, windows inside and out.
- **Empty the cabin:** no phone holders, air fresheners, sun shades, papers or seat covers. Floor mats clean and straight.
- **Plan one location and one time of day for the whole fleet**, so every listing looks like it belongs to the same company.
- **Number plates:** cover them with a neat Aurevon plate card, or blur them before uploading. Visible plates make your cars easier to clone or target.

## 2. Where to shoot

**At your car stand (main photos)**
- Pick the cleanest corner of the yard with a plain background: a painted wall, your signage, or open sky.
- Move other cars, generators, tyres, buckets and anything else out of the frame. Keep staff and visitors out of the shot.
- Ideal: a clean branded backdrop (a wall or banner with "Aurevon Services") that every car is photographed in front of.

**On the road (a lifestyle shot or two)**
- Calabar: Marian Road or the Marina Resort waterfront early on a Sunday morning; Tinapa's wide roads.
- Lagos: quiet estate roads in Lekki or Ikoyi, or a service lane early in the morning.
- Never stop in a live traffic lane or obstruct anyone. Get permission inside private estates.
- Don't photograph near military, police or other restricted government sites.

## 3. Light

- Shoot early morning (7–9 am) or late afternoon (4–6 pm). The low, soft sun makes paint glow.
- **Avoid 11 am–3 pm.** Harsh overhead sun creates dark shadows and glare on the paint.
- A bright overcast day is also good. After rain, wet ground gives nice reflections, but dry the car first.
- Keep the sun behind you or to one side. Don't shoot straight into it.

## 4. Camera settings

- **Landscape (sideways) only.** The app shows photos wide, in a 16:10 frame.
- Use the 1× or 2× lens, never 0.5× (ultra-wide), which distorts the car.
- Hold the phone at headlight height: crouch slightly.
- Leave space around the car (it should fill about 70% of the frame), so nothing gets cropped.
- Turn the front wheels slightly towards the camera and switch on the daytime running lights.
- Wear dark clothing so you don't appear reflected in the doors.
- Keep the horizon level (turn on the grid in camera settings).

## 5. Shot list (per car)

| # | Shot | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Front three-quarter** | The cover photo. Front corner of the car, slightly to one side |
| 2 | Side profile | Whole car side-on, wheels turned straight |
| 3 | Rear three-quarter | Mirror of shot 1 from the back |
| 4 | Dashboard | From the back seat, between the front seats, screen on |
| 5 | Seats | Second row; third row for 7–8 seaters |
| 6 | Boot / luggage space | Tailgate open, empty and clean |
| 7 | Special feature | V-Class and Granvia: sliding door open showing the captain's chairs. Hilux: the load bed. Escalade: the curved screen |
| 8 | Road shot (optional) | The car on a clean Calabar or Lagos road |

## 6. Files

- JPG, at least 1600 px wide, under 8 MB each (the app's upload limit).
- Name them clearly, e.g. `lc300-1-front.jpg`.
- Light editing only: straighten, brighten slightly. No filters, and don't change the car's colour.

## 7. Put them in the app

1. Open **Staff dashboard → Vehicles**. Cars still using stand-in photos are marked **"Stand-in photo"**.
2. Click **Edit listing** on the car, then **Add photos** and select all of its photos.
3. Click **Make cover** on the front three-quarter shot.
4. Delete the old stand-in photo (bin icon), then **Save changes**.

The photo credit under the picture disappears automatically once your own photo is the cover, and the "Stand-in photo" tag clears.
